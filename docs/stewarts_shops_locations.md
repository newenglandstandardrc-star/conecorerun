## User Personas

Three distinct player types have been identified based on the typical Stewart's Shops customer base. Each persona represents a different motivation for playing the game, and the app should be designed to serve all three.

---

### Persona 1 — "The Upstate Regular"
**Name:** Deb M.
**Age:** 42
**Location:** Queensbury, NY
**Occupation:** School administrator
**Household:** Married, two kids (ages 9 and 14)

**About Deb:**
Deb has been stopping at Stewart's her entire life. She knows the staff at her two or three regular locations by name, has a milk punch card in her wallet, and her kids think a Friday night Stewart's ice cream run is a legitimate event. Stewart's isn't a convenience store to her — it's a community institution, and she's quietly proud of it in the way upstate New Yorkers are proud of things that are theirs.

**How she plays:**
Deb isn't racing anyone. She downloads the app because her daughter saw it and got excited. She checks in organically — at the locations she already visits — and slowly realizes she's making a dent in her county without trying. She gets competitive when she notices a neighbor is ahead of her on the county leaderboard. She'd never plan a road trip around the game, but she might take a slightly different route home to hit a location she's close to.

**Goals:**
- Complete her home county (Washington County) at her own pace
- Beat her neighbor Karen on the leaderboard
- Collect the ice cream-themed badges with her daughter

**Frustrations:**
- Doesn't want a complicated app — if it takes more than two taps she's out
- Doesn't want to feel pressured to buy something or compete seriously
- Rural cell service is spotty on her commute

**Devices:** iPhone, uses it constantly but not tech-savvy
**Motivation:** Community pride, casual fun, family activity
**Tagline:** *"I didn't know I was competitive about this until I was."*

---

### Persona 2 — "The Commuter / Blue Collar Worker"
**Name:** Mike T.
**Age:** 34
**Location:** Troy, NY
**Occupation:** Electrician, works job sites across the Capital Region and North Country
**Household:** Single, rents an apartment

**About Mike:**
Mike stops at Stewart's every single morning without fail — large coffee, egg and cheese on a hard roll, gas if he needs it. It's not a choice, it's a routine. He's been to probably 60 or 70 locations just by following work across the region. He's not sentimental about it, but he'd be annoyed if a Stewart's closed near a job site.

**How he plays:**
Mike hears about the game from a coworker and realizes he's already been to a huge chunk of locations without thinking about it. That hooks him immediately. He becomes one of the most naturally competitive players because his job literally takes him to new locations all the time. He checks in during his morning stop, doesn't think much about it, and then gets genuinely invested when he realizes he might actually win something.

**Goals:**
- Dominate the Capital Region leaderboard
- Complete every location he passes through for work without going out of his way
- Eventually complete all of upstate NY

**Frustrations:**
- No time for anything complicated — the check-in needs to be fast
- Spotty signal on rural job sites is a real problem
- Doesn't want to make special trips; wants the game to reward his existing habits

**Devices:** Android, uses it practically not recreationally
**Motivation:** Competition, habit validation, bragging rights with coworkers
**Tagline:** *"I've been playing this game for years without knowing it."*

### Persona 3 — "The Nostalgic Visitor"
**Name:** Jamie R.
**Age:** 29
**Location:** Brooklyn, NY (grew up in Glens Falls, NY)
**Occupation:** Graphic designer at a small agency

**About Jamie:**
Jamie moved to Brooklyn after college and has that specific kind of homesickness that upstate New Yorkers carry — they don't always want to move back, but they miss things fiercely and with great specificity. Stewart's is one of those things. Every time Jamie goes home for a holiday or a summer weekend, a Stewart's stop is non-negotiable. They've made their NYC friends try the ice cream and consider it a personal victory when those friends become converts.

Jamie finds out about the game on social media and immediately screenshots it to send to their group chat of upstate transplants. They play intensely during visits home — planning a whole weekend around hitting as many locations as possible, treating it like a road trip adventure. **Motivation:** Nostalgia, adventure, social sharing, identity
**Tagline:** *"It's not just ice cream. It's where I'm from."*

## Competitive Landscape

Stewart's Shops operates in a crowded convenience store market, but holds a uniquely strong position through regional identity and community loyalty. Players driving between Stewart's locations will constantly encounter competitors — making the game's hidden QR mechanic a natural loyalty reinforcement tool. Here's a high-level view of who else is in the footprint.

---

### National Chains — Volume Players, No Regional Soul

| Chain | NY Locations | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| **7-Eleven** | 839 | Largest chain in NY by far. Mostly downstate but actively expanding upstate. Acquired Speedway in 2020 and is converting those locations. |
| **Speedway** | 256 | Significant upstate presence. Many locations currently being rebranded to 7-Eleven. |
| **Circle K** | ~30 (growing) | Actively expanding — 40 new upstate NY franchise stores planned through The Briad Group. One to watch. |
| **Cumberland Farms** | 78 in NY, 575+ Northeast | Known as "Cumby's." A genuine Northeast regional player with dairy roots ironically similar to Stewart's. Present in both NY and Vermont. |
| **Bolla Market** | 214 in NY | Mostly downstate, limited upstate overlap. |

---

### Regional Players — Closer to Stewart's Territory

| Chain | Footprint | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| **Mirabito** | Upstate NY, northern PA, now Vermont | Operates under multiple sub-brands: Quickway Food Stores, Convenience Express, Manley's Mighty Mart. Highway-focused, grab-and-go model. Recently expanded into Vermont after acquiring stores divested by Stewart's as part of the Jolley deal. |
| **QuickChek** | Hudson Valley, now Capital Region | Known for coffee and made-to-order subs. Moving northward into Stewart's home territory — an emerging and genuine competitive threat. |

---

### Key Takeaways for the Game

- Players driving between Stewart's locations will pass competitors constantly — the game creates a natural "loyalty test" on every route.
- The hidden QR mechanic means players must physically enter a Stewart's, not just drive past it or stop at a competitor for gas.
- Stewart's community identity and regional pride are powerful differentiators that no national chain can replicate — the game leans directly into that.
- As Circle K and 7-Eleven expand upstate, the timing of a loyalty game is actually strategically smart for Stewart's — it deepens emotional attachment before competitors gain more of a foothold.

## App Screens

### Screen 1 — Home screen

The home screen is designed to work across three distinct user contexts: at the gas pump (quick glance, one action), in the register line (30 seconds to browse), and at home (leisurely planning). The hierarchy is built around that priority — the most critical action is always visible without scrolling.

---

#### Layout from top to bottom

**Status bar**
Standard mobile status bar with time, wifi, and battery.

**Header**
- Player name (large) with a muted greeting above it
- Streak badge (flame icon + day count) in Stewart's red
- Player avatar initials circle in red

**GPS proximity alert** *(context-aware, only shown when near a location)*
- Red-tinted banner with map pin icon
- Shows nearest unchecked Stewart's name and distance
- "Go" button navigates to that location
- This is the first thing a player sees when they pull into a Stewart's for gas

**Primary CTA — Scan check-in code**
- Full-width red button, always visible above the fold
- QR code icon + large label
- Works for all three contexts: pump, line, and home

**Progress cards** *(2-column grid)*
- County progress (e.g. Warren Co. · 11/14)
- State progress (e.g. New York · 67/354)
- Full network progress (e.g. 67/407)
- Badges earned (e.g. 8, with colored dots previewing recent badges)
- Each card shows a red progress bar

**Active race card**
- Race name and time remaining
- Current place badge (green)
- Mini leaderboard with progress bars for each competitor
- Player's bar shown in Stewart's red; competitors in neutral gray

**Nearby locations**
- Up to 3 closest Stewart's listed
- Checked-in locations show a green checkmark circle
- Unchecked locations show bonus tags inline:
  - **Pioneer +50** (amber) — low check-in location, worth going out of the way
  - **Loyal +25** (red) — competitor nearby, bonus for choosing Stewart's
- Distance and street shown for each

**Bonuses available today**
- Three bonus mechanic cards stacked:
  - Loyalty bonus — drive past a competitor · +25 pts
  - Pioneer bonus — visit a specific low-traffic location · +50 pts
  - Dairy bonus — buy milk, eggs or ice cream · +15 pts

**Bottom navigation**
Five tabs: Home · Map · Races · Badges · Profile

---

#### Key design decisions

- **Stewart's red** used exclusively for primary actions, active states, and the player's own data — competitors always shown in neutral gray
- **Dark phone frame** grounds the mockup visually; the app itself uses a clean light background
- **Bonus tags appear on location rows** so players can immediately see which stops are worth a detour without navigating elsewhere
- **No scrolling required** to reach the scan button — the most critical pump/line action is always above the fold
- **GPS alert is context-aware** — only appears when the player is physically near a Stewart's, keeping the home view clean for at-home browsing

### Screen 2 — Race detail (friends)

The race detail screen is accessed from the Races tab. It shows the full picture of an active friend race — leaderboard, stage progress, and a live activity feed. Each player gets a consistent color throughout the screen (you = red, friends = blue/teal/gray) so you can scan without reading names.

---

#### Layout from top to bottom

**Header**
- Back arrow + race name + "Live" badge (green)

**Race stats** *(2-column grid)*
- Time remaining
- Current stage name

**Leaderboard**
- Full ranked list of all competitors
- Each row shows: rank number · avatar initials (color-coded) · name · check-in count · progress bar
- Your row is highlighted in Stewart's red with a "X behind" pill showing the gap to first
- Bonus badges shown inline (e.g. "Pioneer x2") to show how players earned their score
- Each player's progress bar uses their unique color

**Stage progress**
- Three stages listed in sequence: County heat · State heat · Full network
- Active stage shown in red with a chevron to drill in
- Future stages shown as locked with a lock icon
- Stages unlock sequentially — you must complete County before State opens

**Recent activity feed**
- Chronological list of every check-in and bonus earned across all players
- Each event shows: player avatar (color-coded) · description · time ago
- Bonus events show a colored point badge (e.g. Pioneer +50, Loyal +25)
- Feed makes the race feel alive — you can see exactly what competitors are doing and where

**Action buttons** *(2-column)*
- Invite friend (red, primary)
- View map (secondary)

**Bottom navigation**
Races tab active (red)

---

#### Key design decisions

- **Per-player color coding** is consistent across leaderboard, progress bars, and activity feed — you never need to read a name twice
- **"14 behind" gap pill** on your row is intentionally uncomfortable — a small competitive nudge to go check in
- **Activity feed is the competitive engine** — seeing a friend earned a Pioneer bonus 3 hours ago tells you exactly where the race is heating up
- **Stage locks** keep the competitive scope clear — everyone follows the same county → state → full network path

### Screen 3 — Race activity feed *(revised)*

The activity feed on the race screen shows competitive progress in stat-only form. No location names, no timestamps, no visit patterns. Competitors see that your score moved — nothing more.

---

#### What competitors see in the feed

- "Mike completed a new location · +1"
- "Mike earned a Pioneer bonus · +50"
- "Mike earned a Loyal bonus · +25"
- "Mike completed Warren County · stage 1 done"

#### What only you see (private)

- Which specific locations you have and haven't visited
- When you visited them
- Time gaps between visits
- Any purchase or dairy bonus detail

This model keeps competition alive — scores go up, badges appear, milestones are celebrated — without revealing the underlying where or when that creates safety risk.

---

#### Persona behavior in a stat-only race

| Persona | What competitors see | What stays private |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Mike T. (commuter) | Steady daily score increases | That he stops every morning near job sites |
| Dave (road tripper) | A sudden jump of 11 points overnight | That he planned a Saturday binge route |
| Sara (nostalgic visitor) | A burst of activity then silence | That she's only in the area on weekend visits |
| Deb (upstate regular) | Slow, consistent progress | That her check-ins are organic family stops |

## Safety and privacy — core design principles

Safety and privacy are not features in this game. They are foundational constraints that shape every design decision. These principles are non-negotiable and must be documented, reviewed, and enforced at every stage of development — before a single line of code is written.

> This section must be reviewed by a qualified domestic violence technology safety advocate before the app ships. The recommended framework is the **NNEDV Safety Net Project** ([techsafety.org](https://www.techsafety.org)), the national gold standard for technology safety in the context of intimate partner violence, stalking, and gender-based violence. Safety Net can be reached at safetynet@nnedv.org or (202) 543-5566.

---

### Why this matters — the threat is real

A survey of domestic violence service providers found that 97% of those they serve report some level of technology-facilitated abuse in their cases. Gaming and social apps are explicitly identified as platforms where this abuse occurs. An app that reveals where someone goes — even with a delay, even in aggregate — can be weaponized by an abuser to track, isolate, or harm a survivor.

This is not a hypothetical edge case. Stewart's Shops operates in rural upstate New York, Vermont, and New Hampshire — areas where survivors may have limited access to services, limited transportation options, and where a predictable check-in pattern at a local store could be enough information to put someone in danger.

---

### The core rule — no live or near-live location sharing

This game must never function as a location tracking tool under any circumstances.

**Hard rules, no exceptions:**
- No real-time location sharing with any other player, ever
- No push notifications to competitors when a player checks in
- No pattern summaries that could reveal a routine (e.g. "tends to visit Tuesday mornings")
- No "last seen near" or proximity indicators of any kind
- A minimum 24-hour delay before any activity appears in any shared feed — and even then, only as a stat event (score change, badge earned), never a location or timestamp
- Check-in history is never exportable or accessible outside the app

---

### What is and isn't public

| Data point | Visible to competitors | Visible to you |
|------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Current score and rank | Yes | Yes |
| Total check-ins completed | Yes | Yes |
| Badges and milestones earned | Yes (type only) | Yes (full detail) |
| County/state/network completion % | Yes | Yes |
| Specific locations visited | No | Yes |
| When locations were visited | No | Yes |
| Time gaps between visits | No | Yes |
| Purchase or dairy bonus detail | No | Yes (honor system) |
| Real-time or recent location | No | No |

---

### Sprint data sharing — opt-in only

Players in a friend sprint may choose to share additional stats with their group. This is entirely optional, clearly explained, and easy to revoke at any time.

**Opt-in design rules:**
- Plain-language consent prompt at sprint join — never buried in settings
- Separate opt-in per sprint, not a global setting
- Revoking opt-in removes your historical sprint data from other players immediately — not just future data
- If you block a player, they see zero data about you even within a shared sprint

**Even with full opt-in, the following are never shared:**
- Specific locations visited
- Visit timestamps or time gaps
- Any purchase-related data
- Real-time or near-real-time location

---

### The quiet exit

Every player has the ability to instantly pause all data sharing without explanation, without quitting the race, and without any visible signal to other players. No confirmation dialog. No reason required. No trace. This feature is designed specifically for anyone who needs to go dark quickly and must be accessible in one tap from anywhere in the app. It must never alert other players that a pause has occurred.

### National framework — NNEDV Safety Net

The **National Network to End Domestic Violence (NNEDV) Safety Net Project** is the definitive national authority on the intersection of technology and domestic violence safety. Their framework covers:

- VAWA, FVPSA, and VOCA confidentiality obligations for technology platforms
- Best practices for app design that intersects with survivor safety
- Guidance on location data, behavioral patterns, and data sharing in consumer apps
- The *Technology Safety & Privacy Toolkit for Survivors* — a mandatory reference for any development team working on this app

**Contact:** safetynet@nnedv.org · 202-543-5566 · [techsafety.org](https://www.techsafety.org)

---

### Development and legal requirements

- A formal privacy and safety review by an NNEDV Safety Net-aligned advocate must be completed before any location or data sharing feature ships
- The app must comply with CCPA and VAWA digital safety provisions
- Compliance with Vermont's Safe at Home Address Confidentiality Program requirements must be reviewed for any account creation flow
- A plain-language privacy policy must be presented at account creation — not at first launch
- No third-party advertising or data sale of any kind — consistent with Stewart's ad-free, community-first brand values
- Any future feature involving location, timing, behavioral data, or social sharing must be reviewed against these principles before development begins
- The development team should complete NNEDV Safety Net training before building any social or location-adjacent feature

### Wellspring partnership — safety as a living value

Rather than safety being an invisible design constraint, the app has an opportunity to make it a visible, active community value through a formal partnership with **Wellspring** — Saratoga County's full provider for victims of sexual assault and domestic violence, and an existing long-term charitable partner of Stewart's Shops through the Holiday Match program.

This isn't a manufactured alignment. Stewart's has supported Wellspring for years. The game's privacy principles are a natural extension of something Stewart's already stands for and funds. A Wellspring partnership turns safety from a design rule into a living part of the game's identity.

**Wellspring** · [wellspringcares.org](https://www.wellspringcares.org)
24-hour hotline: 518-584-8188
Services: Domestic violence, sexual assault, trafficking, elder abuse, teen dating violence — all free and confidential across Saratoga and Washington Counties, NY

#### How charity is surfaced in the app

Rather than a complex points-to-donation mechanic, the app spotlights organizations Stewart's already supports through natural, non-transactional UX moments. This keeps the game fun and legally clean, while staying true to Stewart's community identity.

**Where charities appear:**
- A "Community" card on the home screen, rotating between featured organizations (Wellspring, Holiday Match recipients, local youth programs)
- A milestone moment screen — when a player completes a county or stage, a brief card appears celebrating the achievement and highlighting a local charity in that region
- A dedicated Community section in the app, linking to Stewart's existing giving page at stewartsshops.com/community-giving
- Seasonal moments tied to the Holiday Match program (Thanksgiving through Christmas) where the app acknowledges the campaign and links to Stewart's donation page

**What this is not:**
- Points are never converted to donations
- No transaction or redemption is required to see or engage with charity content
- Players are never pressured or incentivized to give — the information is simply there, the way it is in Stewart's stores

**Regional charity visibility by area:**
- Saratoga / Capital Region → Wellspring
- Vermont → Vermont Network Against Domestic and Sexual Violence
- New Hampshire → NH Coalition Against Domestic and Sexual Violence
- Adirondacks / North Country → Regional Holiday Match recipients
- All regions → stewartsshops.com/community-giving

---

#### Important caveats

- Any charity content, partnership language, or organization references must be reviewed and approved by Stewart's corporate communications before appearing in the app
- Stewart's should not be described as a "safe space" in any official or legal sense without their explicit endorsement — the partnership story is stronger and more honest than a label
- The development team should consult Wellspring's prevention and education team about appropriate in-app language

## Incentives and rewards

The points economy should always feel fun, low-stakes, and authentically Stewart's. The moment points feel like real money, the game stops being a game — players get frustrated, make bad decisions, and the whole thing stops being enjoyable. That's the opposite of what this is for.

**The guiding principle: the stakes should never be high enough for a user to compromise good decision making.**

---

### The currency — Scoops

Points are called **Scoops** — rooted in Stewart's dairy and ice cream identity, immediately understood, impossible to confuse with cash or financial value. You earn Scoops by playing the game. You spend Scoops on Stewart's products. That's it.

**Scoops have no cash value. Ever.**
- Not redeemable for cash or gift cards
- Not transferable between players
- Not purchasable with real money
- Redeemable for Stewart's products only

This keeps the game clean across New York, Vermont, and New Hampshire promotional and loyalty program law, and ensures it never drifts toward regulated gaming territory.

---

### Earning Scoops

| Action | Scoops earned |
|--------|--------------|
| Check in at any location | 10 |
| Pioneer bonus (low check-in location) | +50 |
| Loyal bonus (near a competitor) | +25 |
| Dairy bonus (honor system tap) | +15 |
| Complete your home county | +100 |
| Complete a friend sprint | +75 |
| Complete a full state | +500 |
| Complete the full network | +2,000 |
| Seasonal challenge completion | +150 |

---

### Spending Scoops

Redeemed at any Stewart's register — no app needed at the counter, just a simple code or loyalty card tap. Rewards are modest and meaningful, not extravagant. A free coffee or a scoop of ice cream is well within what Stewart's already gives away through community events and promotions.

| Scoops | Reward |
|--------|--------|
| 50 | Free coffee (any size) |
| 100 | Free single scoop ice cream |
| 200 | Free hand-packed pint |
| 400 | Free milk punch card (10 stamps) |
| 1,000 | Free ice cream cake |
| 2,500 | Grand reward — TBD with Stewart's corporate |

---

### Digital badges and recognition

Always free. Never require a purchase or Scoop redemption. These are about identity, pride, and the joy of the game.

| Milestone | Badge |
|-----------|-------|
| First check-in | "First Scoop" |
| Complete home county | County badge |
| Pioneer bonus earned | "Pioneer" |
| Loyal bonus earned | "Loyal" |

| Top 10 all-time leaderboard | Special visible profile badge |

---

### How the personas experience rewards

| Persona | Natural earning pattern | How they redeem |
|---------|------------------------|-----------------|
| Deb (upstate regular) | Earns slowly and organically — ice cream runs, milk pickups | Redeems a free pint with her kids after finishing her county. High joy, zero stress. |
| Mike (commuter) | Accumulates steadily on daily commute stops | Redeems a free coffee without thinking twice. Feels like a natural perk of his routine. |
| Jamie (nostalgic visitor) | Earns in bursts during upstate visits | Saves Scoops for a visit home, redeems a free ice cream cake. Feels like the game gave them something real. |

---

### What to avoid

- Never frame Scoops as having monetary value in any marketing or UX copy
- Never allow Scoops to expire without at least 90 days notice to the player (New York loyalty program law requirement)
- Never require a purchase to earn Scoops — the QR scan alone is sufficient
- Never create a mechanic where losing a race costs a player Scoops they've already earned
- Never make the leaderboard feel so high-stakes that players feel pressure to visit unsafe locations, drive recklessly, or compromise their judgment to compete

## Stewart's corporate perspective

*The following represents a hypothetical internal perspective from Stewart's Shops corporate — including marketing, legal, operations, finance, and the Dake family — evaluating this game concept as a potential sponsorship or endorsement decision. It is written in good faith to stress-test the concept and identify what would need to be true for Stewart's to say yes.*

---

### What we'd love about this

The community angle is genuinely compelling. We already give $12 million a year to local causes. We already partner with Wellspring. We already run Holiday Match. A game that makes our stores feel like destinations rather than pit stops — and that drives traffic to our smaller, rural locations that need it most — is exactly the kind of thing we'd be proud to put our name on.

The Pioneer bonus mechanic alone is worth a serious conversation. Getting players to seek out our Harrisville or Indian Lake location? Nobody has ever made that feel exciting before.

The Scoops economy is clean and familiar to us. We already have a milk punch card. We understand loyalty mechanics. A free coffee or a scoop of ice cream as a reward is nothing we haven't done before — and it's entirely on-brand.

The safety framework is something we'd point to with genuine pride. Our motto is "We Are Closer To You." An app that takes that seriously — that actively protects vulnerable users and partners with Wellspring — feels like us.

---

### What would keep us up at night

**Brand control is everything.**
We are a privately held, family-owned company. The Dake family has spent 80 years building this brand carefully, store by store, community by community. We do not move fast and break things. If this app has a bad day — a data breach, a safety incident, a viral complaint about a player being followed to a store — that's our name on it. Not a startup's name. Ours. Our risk appetite here is very low.

**We don't have an app infrastructure.**
We have a website, SMS alerts, and a store locator. Building or sponsoring a full gaming app is a significant operational leap. Who maintains it? Who handles customer support when a player's Scoops don't show up? Who manages the QR codes in 400+ stores, replaces them when they're damaged or discovered too easily, and rotates their placement? This is not a small operational ask on top of running a dairy, a distribution network, and 400 convenience stores.

**The QR code placement is a real staffing concern.**
We'd need every store manager across three states to understand the game, maintain the hidden QR code, rotate its placement, and handle customers who can't find it or are frustrated. That's 400+ managers who need training and buy-in. Our partners — that's what we call our employees — are already running a lot. Adding a game mechanic to their daily responsibilities needs to feel manageable, not burdensome.

**We are not a tech company.**
Any vendor or development partner who builds this needs to be vetted carefully. We've never opened our store systems to a third-party app integration. The register redemption mechanic alone — Scoops redeemable at the counter — requires touching our POS system. That's a significant integration with real operational and security risk.

**The competitor proximity feature makes us nervous.**
We appreciate the concept of the Loyal bonus — drive past a competitor to reach us — but we have never publicly named competitors in our marketing. We are quietly confident. We don't do trash talk. Any in-app language that names Circle K, Cumberland Farms, or 7-Eleven would need to be removed or handled extremely carefully. The bonus can exist without naming anyone.

**Underage players.**
Our stores sell alcohol, tobacco, and lottery tickets. We are very careful about age-related compliance. A game that drives foot traffic into our stores needs to think carefully about whether it's appropriate for minors, and what guardrails exist. If a 14-year-old is checking in at stores alone late at night because of a game we sponsored, that's a problem we own.

**Liability for player behavior.**
If someone gets in a car accident rushing between locations to beat a friend in a sprint, are we exposed? We'd need legal to review this carefully. The principle that "the stakes should never be high enough to compromise good decision making" is exactly right — but we'd want that expressed in the terms of service in very explicit language, and we'd want the game design reviewed to ensure nothing inadvertently creates urgency or reckless behavior.

---

### What would stop us from going live

**No trusted development partner.**
We wouldn't greenlight this without a vetted, experienced development partner who has built location-based apps before, understands loyalty program law across NY, VT, and NH, and can demonstrate a credible security and privacy framework.

**No clear operational owner inside Stewart's.**
Someone at corporate needs to own this — a real person with a real budget and real authority. Without a champion inside the building, this doesn't survive contact with our organizational structure.

**Any feature that exposes location data in real time.**
Full stop. We agree with every word of the safety section in this document — but we'd want an independent legal and safety review before signing off. Not because we don't trust the document, but because this is exactly the kind of thing that needs a second set of eyes that isn't the person who designed the game.

**If the QR mechanic creates customer complaints at scale.**
The hidden QR has to feel fun to find, not punishing to miss. If customers feel frustrated, tricked, or like they're being sent on a wild goose chase inside our stores, that reflects on us directly.

**If it isn't exclusively and unmistakably Stewart's.**
This must be a Stewart's game, not a generic check-in game that happens to include our logo. The Scoops, the Pioneer bonus, the Cannonball Run spirit — all of it needs to feel like it could only exist because of who we are. The moment it feels like a white-label product, we're out.

---

### What we'd want to see before saying yes

- A full working prototype tested in a small number of stores — 10 to 20 locations in Saratoga County — before any broader rollout
- A clear data privacy and security audit from a qualified third party
- Legal review of the Scoops economy across NY, VT, and NH
- Wellspring's sign-off on how they're represented in the app
- A staffing and operations plan for QR code management at store level
- A terms of service that explicitly addresses player safety, driving behavior, and age restrictions
- A defined sunset plan — what happens to players' Scoops if the game ends

---

### Our honest bottom line

We love the spirit of this. It feels like us. The community values, the dairy roots, the rural pride, the safety commitment — all of it. But we are a conservative, family-owned company and we would move slowly and carefully.

The right path is a quiet pilot in Saratoga County, built with a trusted partner, with Wellspring involved from day one, and a clear decision point after six months about whether to expand.

If someone brought us a polished pilot proposal with a credible tech partner, a safety review, and a staffing plan — we'd take the meeting.

## Game Concept — ConeCoreRun *(beta name)*

A real-world racing game inspired by the Cannonball Run, built around visiting every Stewart's Shops location. Ideally developed in partnership with Stewart's corporate, leveraging their community-oriented brand identity.

---

### Core Concept
Players race to visit Stewart's Shops locations across a self-defined territory or the full network. The first to complete each stage wins that heat. Designed to be fun, low-barrier, and deeply tied to the Stewart's community spirit.

---

### Check-In Mechanic
- A **hidden QR code** is placed somewhere inside each store (not at the register, not obvious — players have to walk in and look around)
- No purchase required to scan, but players must physically enter and browse the store
- QR code placement **rotates periodically** (e.g. near the ice cream counter one week, by the coffee station the next), keeping it fresh and rewarding explorers
- Stewart's staff are in on the secret, which fits their tight-knit employee culture

---

### Territory Definition — User-Drawn Runs *(key innovation)*

Rather than being confined to administrative county or state boundaries, players **draw their own run** on a map during onboarding. A draggable circle (radius) centered on their location defines their starting territory — the stores inside that circle become their initial heat.

**Why this matters:**
- Smaller footprint = more completable = faster wins = better for casual players (Deb)
- Larger footprint = prestige territory = bragging rights for serious players (Mike)
- No two runs are identical — each player's game reflects their real geography and life
- Players are naturally invested in their territory because they defined it themselves

**How it works:**
- During onboarding, the player sees a dot map of all nearby Stewart's locations
- They drag a circle to define their run — stores inside the circle are their first heat
- The circle shows a live count of included locations as it's dragged
- Minimum: 3 locations. No maximum — players can circle the whole network if they want
- After onboarding, the run is expandable but never shrinkable (you can't un-visit a store)

---

### Home Base — First Store Visited

The **first QR code a player scans becomes their home base** — their personal flag in the game. This is the store they start from, the store they're most attached to, and the store that defines their identity on the map.

**Home base mechanics:**
- Home base is displayed prominently on the player's profile and map
- Multiple players can share the same home base store — this is by design, not a problem
- Shared home base stores create natural local rivalry without requiring explicit matchmaking
- The home base is a social anchor: "I'm a 110 Hoosick Street player" is a real identity

---

### Capture the Flag *(emerging mechanic — in development)*

Home base introduces a capture-the-flag layer that rewards territorial loyalty and drives repeat visits to specific stores.

**Current thinking (not finalized):**
- Your home base store is your flag
- Other players checking into your home base store create competitive pressure
- Defending your flag means checking in more frequently — driving real repeat foot traffic
- A "contested" store could show a special indicator on the map
- Potential siege mechanic: if a rival checks in at your home base N times without a response, they earn a territory bonus
- This layer is opt-in — casual players (Deb) never need to engage with it

**Stewart's business case for CTF:**
- Incentivizes loyalty to individual locations, not just the brand generally
- Drives repeat visits to specific stores — exactly what franchise operators want
- Creates a reason to rush back to a particular Stewart's, not just any Stewart's

> *Note: CTF mechanics require additional design work before implementation. The home base concept is confirmed; the competitive pressure layer is still being thought through.*

---

### Stages / Heats
1. **Heat 1 — Your run:** First to complete every store inside your drawn territory
2. **Heat 2 — Region:** First to complete all locations in a broader area (county, state)
3. **Heat 3 — Full Network:** First to visit every Stewart's location across NY, VT & NH

### Seasonal Races
Timed events tied to local culture and the Stewart's calendar:
- Complete all Saratoga Springs locations before track season ends
- Complete all Adirondack locations during foliage season
- Winter challenge: hit all Vermont locations during ski season

---

### Family Mode — Scoopers

Players can add family members to their account as **Scoopers** — co-pilots who participate in the game experience without being registered contest entrants.

**Key rules:**
- Main account holder must be 18+ (legal contest requirement across NY, VT, NH)
- Scoopers under 18 get full map access, check-in celebrations, and badges — but Scoops and prizes stay on the main account
- Scoopers 18+ are encouraged to create their own accounts
- Family teams are named (e.g. "The Troy Crew") — the team name is the only public identity
- Scoopers can be added, edited, or removed at any time from Profile → Team Settings

**Why this matters for Stewart's:**
- Deb's Friday night ice cream run with her kids becomes a family scavenger hunt
- Kids aged 8-17 are engaged and having fun without creating legal contest eligibility issues
- Families as a unit become loyal to specific stores — the home base mechanic applies to the whole family

---

### Social & Competitive Features
- **Friend sprints:** Race your crew head-to-head with agreed stage and timeline
- **Stranger matchmaking:** Get paired with other racers across the region
- **Team names only:** Real names and emails never appear publicly — ever
- **Leaderboards:** Per run, per region, full network, fastest-ever completion
- **Player accounts:** Track personal progress, badges earned, and race history
- **Badges:** Awarded for completing runs, regions, seasonal events, and milestone check-ins

---

### Age & Legal Requirements
- **Minimum age:** 18 across New York, Vermont, and New Hampshire
- **Minimum registration data:** First name, last name, email, date of birth, state, agreement to official rules
- **NY registration:** Any sweepstakes with prizes over $5,000 must be registered and bonded with the NY Department of State at least 30 days before launch
- **No purchase necessary:** The QR scan requires no purchase — legally required across all three states
- **Marketing to minors:** The game mechanic (scavenger hunt, badges) must not be marketed as targeting children — Family mode is positioned as a parental choice, not child-targeted advertising
- *Legal counsel required before launch*

---

### Business Case for Stewart's
- Drives foot traffic to every location, including smaller and more rural stores
- User-defined territories naturally surface underserved locations as "Pioneer" opportunities
- Home base mechanic creates loyalty to individual stores, not just the brand
- CTF mechanic incentivizes repeat visits to specific locations
- No forced purchase — consistent with Stewart's generous, community-first values
- Staff involvement (knowing where the QR is hidden) builds morale and store pride
